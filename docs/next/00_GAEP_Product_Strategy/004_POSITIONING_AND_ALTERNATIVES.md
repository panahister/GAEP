# Positioning and Alternatives

## Status and purpose

This Proposed document defines the comparisons GAEP must survive before its scope is approved. It is not a competitive intelligence report, market validation, commercial positioning approval, or assertion that GAEP is superior to the alternatives below.

The existing external-project registry evaluates possible integrations and inspirations. It does not answer whether an organization should adopt GAEP rather than improve its current engineering practices or compose existing capabilities.

## Candidate category

**Category hypothesis:** GAEP is a portable governance specification and engineering-memory model for consequential human-AI engineering change.

This wording is intended to distinguish GAEP from:

- an AI coding agent;
- a prompt or instruction library;
- a universal software-development lifecycle;
- a document management system;
- an enterprise architecture repository;
- a backlog, design, source-control, test, delivery, or operations system;
- a policy engine by itself;
- an autonomous approval system;
- a mandatory centralized database of organizational truth.

The category remains open because users may understand GAEP more clearly as a governance layer, specification, workspace contract, operating model, or product family. Research must test comprehension without teaching participants the entire corpus first.

## Candidate positioning statement

> For engineering organizations using AI across consequential changes and fragmented sources, GAEP provides vendor-neutral semantics for trusted context, impact, evidence, accountable decisions, and portable traceability. Unlike provider-specific histories or disconnected lifecycle tools, GAEP is intended to preserve governed meaning across tools while allowing authoritative assets to remain in their proper systems.

Every phrase above is a hypothesis. In particular, *trusted*, *portable*, and *unlike* require defined evidence.

## Alternative landscape

| Alternative | Strengths to respect | Potential gap relevant to GAEP | Likely relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do nothing or rely on expert memory | No migration or additional ceremony | Continuity and reconstructability may depend on individuals | Valid baseline for low-cost, low-risk work |
| Repository documentation plus review rules | Local, inspectable, widely understood | Authority, evidence, lifecycle, and cross-tool semantics may be inconsistent | Foundation or simpler substitute |
| Source-control review and branch protection | Strong change diff, identity, and merge controls | Merge does not necessarily equal product, architecture, security, or risk approval | Complement and authoritative source |
| Agent- or provider-specific instructions and approvals | Tight integration and rapid provider innovation | Memory and control may not transfer across providers | Adapter target or adequate substitute |
| Backlog and application-lifecycle tools | Mature workflow, ownership, reporting, integrations | Engineering context and evidence may remain fragmented across systems | Authoritative external system and complement |
| Developer portals and internal platforms | Discoverability, golden paths, service ownership | May not govern decision/evidence chains across AI work | Complement, integration, or competing platform layer |
| Architecture and design repositories | Specialized visual and decision capabilities | May not connect implementation, AI execution, assurance, and human approval portably | Authoritative external system and complement |
| Policy-as-code and compliance automation | Deterministic enforcement and continuous controls | May not represent product intent, uncertainty, decision rationale, or complete context | Core control complement |
| Knowledge management, search, graph, or retrieval systems | Discovery and organizational memory | Retrieval relevance does not establish authority, approval, or permission | Complement or realization component |
| Security, assurance, and audit platforms | Specialized evidence and control models | May focus on one domain rather than end-to-end engineering change | Complement or profile-specific substitute |
| Process consulting and review boards | Human adaptation and contextual judgment | Knowledge may not become portable or continuously executable | Adoption method or substitute |
| A composed existing-tool workflow | Uses current investments and user habits | Integration and semantic gaps may remain | Primary build-versus-compose comparison |

The table describes categories, not current vendor capabilities. Any named vendor evaluation must use current primary evidence and record version, scope, license, cost, limitations, and exit path.

## Proposed differentiation hypotheses

### 1. Authority-aware engineering memory

GAEP proposes that context selection should preserve authority, scope, state, provenance, conflict, and omission rather than treating retrieved content as equivalent text.

**Evidence needed:** comparative tasks showing material difference from ordinary repository search or retrieval.

### 2. Decision and evidence continuity across tools

GAEP proposes portable semantics that survive changes in AI provider, workspace realization, or external-system adapter.

**Evidence needed:** provider or realization substitution without loss or silent reinterpretation of a governed workflow.

### 3. Risk-proportionate profiles over one universal ceremony

GAEP proposes stable Core invariants with selectable profiles that can reduce low-risk burden while preserving consequential controls.

**Evidence needed:** comparable low- and high-risk scenarios with justified differences and no silent weakening.

### 4. Connected human-AI accountability

GAEP proposes a chain from intent and context through AI claims, evidence, human challenge, authorization, and later outcome.

**Evidence needed:** improved decision reconstruction and earlier useful findings without rubber stamping.

### 5. Source federation instead of compulsory centralization

GAEP proposes that external authorities may remain authoritative while portable references expose identity, version, ownership, state, and trace.

**Evidence needed:** a real cross-system workflow that avoids uncontrolled duplicate truth.

## Reasons an organization should not choose GAEP

An honest positioning assessment must identify disqualifiers. GAEP should not be selected when:

- the relevant work is low consequence and existing practices are sufficient;
- there is no owner for governed knowledge or decisions;
- the organization cannot access the authoritative sources needed by the workflow;
- the required burden exceeds the expected value;
- a simpler existing-tool composition satisfies the job;
- the organization seeks autonomous consequential approval;
- the proposed adoption would centralize authority contrary to organizational need;
- legal, privacy, security, labor, or contractual obligations cannot be satisfied;
- portability is not valuable enough to justify abstraction and conformance cost.

## Complement-versus-replacement policy hypothesis

GAEP should normally complement existing systems by owning portable semantics, bindings, and evidence of cross-system decisions. It should replace an incumbent activity only when the replacement is explicit, measured, reversible, and beneficial.

For each artifact or function, the product strategy must decide one of:

- authoritative in GAEP;
- authoritative externally and referenced by GAEP;
- derived or cached with declared freshness;
- temporarily duplicated for migration with an owner and end condition;
- not represented because it is not applicable.

## Build, buy, compose, or stop criteria

Before adding a GAEP capability, compare:

- user job and problem evidence;
- constitutional and conformance necessity;
- strength of existing alternatives;
- interoperability and semantic fit;
- security, privacy, and authority model;
- adoption and migration burden;
- maintenance, support, and total cost;
- reversibility and exit;
- differentiation value;
- evidence that the capability belongs in Core, a profile, a realization, an adapter, or nowhere.

The default is not to build. A capability may be valuable and still belong to an external authoritative system.

## Audience-specific value hypotheses

| Audience | Candidate message | Evidence obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering sponsor | Adopt AI faster without losing accountable engineering control | Outcome, risk, and total-cost evidence |
| Change owner | Reach a decision-ready state without reconstructing every source manually | Time, completeness, and repeat-use evidence |
| Engineer | Receive applicable trusted context and explicit constraints | Re-explanation, correction, and usability evidence |
| Reviewer or approver | Review the material delta, evidence, and unknowns | Finding quality, attention, and latency evidence |
| Steward | Maintain portable meaning without becoming a manual integration team | Maintenance and migration burden evidence |

## Product naming recommendation

The following naming package is a **recommended candidate**, not an Approved name, Baseline designation, trademark conclusion, public claim authorization, or repository-wide rename instruction:

| Element | Recommended candidate | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical name | **Governed AI Engineering Platform** | Preserve the established `GAEP` identity while naming the durable product concern: governed human-AI engineering across the Product-to-Operations span. |
| Acronym | **GAEP** | Stable short identity. |
| Descriptor | **An evidence-driven, adaptive product-to-operations engineering system.** | Explain lifecycle reach and evidence orientation without claiming a universal fixed method. |
| Optional tagline | **From product intent to operational evidence.** | Short explanatory copy only; not a normative product boundary or performance claim. |
| Recommendation state | `recommended-candidate` | Human review remains required. |
| Approval state | `not-approved` | No naming authority is created by this document. |

**Decision status:** Proposed — awaiting explicit Product Owner acceptance.

`Governed` is deliberate: AI participation, claims, decisions, and effects remain bounded by visible authority and evidence. `Engineering` covers Product discovery, architecture, design, backlog, implementation, assurance, release, operations, and controlled evolution; it is not limited to coding or to engineering AI systems. `Enterprise` remains an important applicability context, but making it part of the name would unnecessarily imply that GAEP excludes smaller or non-enterprise contexts. Adding `Product` could wrongly imply that non-Product Engineering Initiatives are unsupported. The acronym is already embedded across documents, identifiers, packages, commands, and user-facing surfaces, so a rename would create broad migration cost without current evidence of proportionate value.

### Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Useful signal | Reason not recommended as the canonical candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Governance AI Enterprise Platform | Retains the acronym and signals organizational use | “Governance AI” is grammatically and semantically ambiguous; “Enterprise” may incorrectly narrow applicability to organization size or suggest an ERP category. |
| Governed AI Product and Engineering Platform | Makes Product work explicit | It is longer, weakens acronym continuity, and may imply that Product is a separate product class rather than one concern in the complete engineering span. |
| Governed AI Engineering Lifecycle Platform | Makes lifecycle coverage explicit | It may imply a fixed prescriptive lifecycle and understate GAEP's evidence, authority, state, and operating-system concerns. |
| Retain the established name and add the descriptor | Preserves continuity while clarifying scope | **Recommended candidate** because it creates the least migration burden and best fits the current vendor-, tool-, architecture-, provider-, and methodology-neutral boundary. |

### Naming claim boundary

The recommended name and descriptor may be used inside candidate documentation when their state is kept visible. They SHALL NOT be represented as Approved, Baseline, legally cleared, trademark-cleared, validated market positioning, or authorization for public or commercial publication. Category comprehension, competitor differentiation, trademark review, localization, and audience testing remain downstream decision work.

### Naming drift inventory

P01 found the canonical expansion `Governed AI Engineering Platform` consistently embedded across the principal current and proposed authority surfaces. The evaluated alternatives are not current aliases and must not be introduced as expansions. Generic uses of “GAEP platform,” “engineering platform,” or “enterprise” describe a context, not a second canonical name. Historical or implementation labels such as `Pre-Figma` are lifecycle/tool terminology issues, not alternative Product names, and P01 does not rewrite them.

Any later incorrect expansion, acronym replacement, capitalization rule, descriptor change, package rename, command rename, or public-brand migration must be inventoried by exact consumer and changed through a separately reviewed migration plan. P01 creates no rename instruction.

## Claims GAEP must avoid

Until separately evidenced and approved, GAEP must not claim that it:

- guarantees correctness, compliance, security, or safe AI;
- eliminates hallucination, incidents, rework, or professional disagreement;
- is suitable for every organization or engineering change;
- has achieved product-market fit or enterprise readiness;
- is automatically vendor-neutral because its prose avoids vendor names;
- replaces professional judgment or lawful authority;
- creates a complete source of truth merely by recording links;
- produces value because its specification is comprehensive;
- is open, commercial, certified, or standardized before those decisions exist.
