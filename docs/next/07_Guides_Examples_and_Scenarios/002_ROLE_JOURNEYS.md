# Role Journeys and Experience Requirements

## Candidate status

Every journey below is a target-experience hypothesis. Present-tense wording describes the proposed experience to test; it does not claim that a current GAEP product or runtime provides the capability. Pilot evidence must validate comprehension, usefulness, accessibility, burden and trust before the experience becomes an approved product requirement.

## Sponsor

The sponsor needs a short explanation of problem, target users, value, burden, risk, funding, ownership, alternatives and stop conditions. The sponsor should not need to read the complete specification to understand the investment decision.

## Change author or technical lead

In the target experience, the author would begin with change intent rather than a catalog of every possible artifact. A future conforming experience should resolve the smallest applicable workflow, reuse existing authority, expose missing information, and produce a reviewable delta. Evaluation must test whether the author can see which work is new, which existing work is replaced, and why each requested obligation applies.

## Engineer using AI assistance

The target experience would give the engineer bounded, classified, provenance-aware context and a clear effect boundary. Evaluation must test whether the engineer can distinguish approved constraints from generated suggestions, understand stop conditions, challenge incorrect context, and continue manually if AI support is unavailable.

## Reviewer

The target experience would give the reviewer the exact changed subject and revisions, impact, evidence, uncertainty, conflicts, prior decisions and requested outcome. Evaluation must test whether meaningful differences can be prioritized over full-document rereading while exposing attention required, deadlines, delegation and conflicts of interest.

## Approver or authority

The target experience should let an approver respond only within actual authority using the controlled Approval model. Evaluation must test whether scope, conditions, expiry, consequences and unresolved Obligations are unmistakable and whether a completed response remains distinct from the aggregate Approval Determination and any Authorization Grant.

## Workspace or specification steward

The target experience should expose broken references, stale evidence, profile conflicts, compatibility changes, expiring exceptions, unsupported adapters and unowned resources to the steward. Evaluation must measure maintenance burden and test whether it can trigger simplification or retirement.

## Auditor or independent assessor

The target experience should let an assessor reconstruct who knew what, from which revisions, under which rules, with which evidence, when a decision or effect occurred. Evaluation must test whether the assessor can distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence and identify corrected or superseded records.

## Affected employee or non-user

The target experience should let an affected person understand what activity data would be collected, its purpose, access, retention, permitted uses and appeal path. Evaluation must verify that pilot telemetry is not silently repurposed for individual performance ranking.

## Small-organization journey

One person may hold several roles, but the binding shows role composition rather than pretending separation. Independent review is reserved for risk conditions that justify it. The lightweight path limits required fields, handoffs and review time.

## Exit journey

The target experience should let an organization pause runtime automation, export authoritative governance state, retain required records, remove provider integrations and continue manually. This remains an unvalidated portability hypothesis; exit testing must show that prior decisions remain interpretable.
