# GAEP Constitution

**Governed AI Engineering Platform (GAEP)**\
**Document ID:** GAEP-FND-001\
**Version:** 1.0\
**Status:** Draft\
**Authority:** Highest-level platform law\
**Change Class:** Constitutional

## Preamble

Artificial intelligence can accelerate product discovery, analysis, design, implementation, testing, documentation, and operations. Acceleration alone does not create consistency, accountability, architectural integrity, organizational memory, or trust.

GAEP exists to govern the participation of AI across the complete product-engineering lifecycle while preserving accountable human ownership. This Constitution establishes the durable laws against which every GAEP capability, repository, workflow, command, skill, agent, integration, and decision must be evaluated.

## Article I — Mission

GAEP shall become a governed, context-aware, vendor-neutral engineering workspace in which organizational knowledge, human expertise, and artificial intelligence collaborate to create and evolve Engineering Initiatives.

An **Engineering Initiative** is GAEP's generic unit of engineering work. A Product is one valid initiative type, not the universal abstraction for all workflows.

GAEP shall optimize for long-term engineering integrity, not short-term content or code generation.

## Article II — Scope of Governance

GAEP may govern product and non-product work, including:

- discovery, requirements, scope, and research;
- products, services, platforms, modules, clients, libraries, APIs, integrations, workers, and data capabilities;
- infrastructure, DevOps, security, observability, migration, modernization, refactoring, defect, and technical-debt initiatives;
- business, product, system, security, data, integration, deployment, and implementation architecture;
- UX research, journeys, design systems, visual assets, prototypes, and UI design when applicable;
- backlog, acceptance, Test Methodology Decisions, implementation, release, and operational evidence;
- controlled change, learning, and governed organizational reuse.

Lifecycle activities and artifacts shall be activated through an **Applicability Decision** based on scope, risk, dependency, policy, quality requirement, or engineering necessity. GAEP shall not require a phase, artifact, tool, method, or approval solely because the platform supports it.

## Article III — Human Accountability and Explicit Approval

1. Accountability remains human.
2. AI may discover, classify, analyze, challenge, recommend, generate, transform, validate, and automate within granted authority.
3. AI may not declare its own work approved or finalize consequential decisions where human approval is required.
4. Decisions affecting initiative scope, architecture, technology, security, identity, authorization, test adequacy, risk acceptance, release, production, or official organizational assets require an identified accountable human when applicable.
5. Delegation of execution does not delegate accountability.
6. **Explicit Approval** shall be attributable, version-specific, scoped, and recorded. It must not be inferred from silence, generation, file presence, or merge status.

## Article IV — Governance Before Automation

1. Automation shall operate inside defined policy, applicability, lifecycle, state, authority, and evidence boundaries.
2. An action that cannot be governed reliably shall not be automated merely because it is technically possible.
3. **Risk-Proportionate Governance** shall reflect business criticality, security and data sensitivity, regulatory exposure, architectural impact, blast radius, operational risk, irreversibility, and cost of failure.
4. High-impact or irreversible actions require stronger controls than low-risk, reversible analysis.
5. Exceptions shall be explicit, time-bounded, owned, traceable, and auditable.

## Article V — Repository as Authoritative Engineering Memory

1. The repository is the authoritative home of approved engineering knowledge within its declared scope.
2. Code is one class of artifact, not the entire engineering memory.
3. Material Initiative Classifications, Applicability Decisions, requirements, **Architecture Assets**, Technology Profiles, Assurance Strategies, identity and authorization decisions, Test Evidence, approvals, rejected alternatives, and supersession history shall be captured in durable artifacts.
4. External authoritative assets may remain in approved systems when their identity, version, ownership, status, and trace relationships are repository-visible.
5. Temporary conversations and provider memory shall not silently become authoritative.
6. The repository shall preserve enough history to explain what changed, why, by whom, under which authority, with what evidence, and with which downstream effects.

## Article VI — Context Before Generation and Rigid Process

1. AI output quality depends on relevant, trusted, current, and applicable context.
2. GAEP shall favor structured context engineering over elaborate one-off prompts or a rigid universal process.
3. Context shall be selected by Engineering Initiative, objective, applicability, state, authority, trace relationships, information classification, and risk.
4. Context shall distinguish authoritative facts from proposals, assumptions, examples, external references, and generated working data.
5. Missing or conflicting context shall be disclosed, not concealed through confident generation.

## Article VII — Traceability by Design

1. Every material artifact shall have an identifiable origin, owner, state, version, and purpose.
2. Initiative intent shall be traceable through applicable requirements, architecture, security, assurance, implementation, verification, release, and operational outcomes.
3. Applicability Decisions, including Not Applicable and Deferred decisions where material, shall be traceable.
4. Requirements, acceptance criteria, test cases, automated tests, coverage evidence, Test Evidence, and approval shall remain connected.
5. Decisions and approvals shall be traceable to the evidence considered.
6. Traceability shall support impact analysis, navigation, audit, living architecture, and organizational learning.

## Article VIII — Explicit Engineering State

1. Governed work shall have explicit initiative, applicability, lifecycle, artifact, architecture, assurance, approval, change, and execution state.
2. Agents shall determine the current state before acting.
3. State transitions shall have defined preconditions, authority, outputs, and evidence.
4. No agent shall infer approval, readiness, or applicability from silence, file presence, or prior generation.
5. The platform shall reject or stop actions that are invalid in the current state.

## Article IX — Architecture Before Implementation

1. Applicable implementation shall conform to an intentional architecture or an explicitly approved, bounded experiment.
2. Applicable boundaries, responsibilities, contracts, quality attributes, trust boundaries, identity model, data ownership, topology, and constraints shall be resolved to the depth required before they are encoded accidentally in implementation.
3. GAEP shall not prescribe one universal architecture style. Modular monoliths, microservices, synchronous or asynchronous integration, monorepos or polyrepos, native or cross-platform clients, and other styles require contextual **Architecture Decisions**.
4. Each major implementation unit shall have an explicitly resolved **Technology Profile** before implementation.
5. Implementation feedback may challenge architecture but shall change it through controlled governance.
6. Generated code shall not become de facto architecture by default.

## Article X — Test-First Assurance and Evidence Before Confidence

1. Claims of quality, correctness, security, readiness, or completion require evidence proportional to risk.
2. Before applicable implementation, GAEP shall resolve the **Assurance Strategy**, Test Methodology Decision, acceptance conditions, required test levels, coverage expectations, automation obligations, and human approval needs.
3. No single test methodology, representation, test level, or coverage percentage is universally sufficient or mandatory.
4. Test Evidence may include automated results, reviews, analyses, simulations, measurements, manual observations, approvals, and operational evidence.
5. AI confidence is not evidence, and manual approval does not replace applicable repeatable testing.
6. Missing evidence shall be identified as a gap.
7. Baselines and releases shall retain supporting Test Evidence and accepted risk.

## Article XI — Controlled Change and Re-evaluation

1. Change is expected and shall be governed.
2. Material changes require intent, impact analysis, challenge, review, approval, implementation, validation, and trace updates.
3. Baselines shall not be silently overwritten.
4. A changed decision shall trigger **Change-Driven Re-evaluation** of affected requirements, Architecture Assets, Technology Profiles, interfaces, data, identity, authorization, tests, deployment, observability, risk, approvals, and documentation.
5. Emergency paths may be faster but shall preserve ownership, evidence, security, rollback, and retrospective review.

## Article XII — Governed Reuse, Organizational Assets, and Learning

1. Existing approved knowledge and organizational assets shall be considered before new assets are created.
2. **Organizational Boilerplates**, starter kits, templates, patterns, commands, skills, policies, and reference implementations are governed, versioned assets with ownership, applicability, provenance, and evidence of fitness.
3. AI must not silently invent, substitute, modify, or represent an unofficial boilerplate as organization-approved.
4. Creating or changing an official Organizational Boilerplate requires explicit human authority and consumer-impact analysis.
5. Reuse shall not override contextual fitness; unsuitable assets shall be adapted through approval or rejected explicitly.
6. Validated learning should improve organizational knowledge without turning local observations into universal rules automatically.

## Article XIII — Technology, Vendor, Architecture, Tool, and Method Neutrality

1. GAEP shall remain conceptually independent of any AI model, vendor, IDE, cloud, programming language, framework, database, cache, broker, repository host, architecture style, or test methodology.
2. Technology may be selected at enterprise, portfolio, initiative, system, bounded-context, service, client, module, workload, or deployment-unit level.
3. Different implementation units may use different technologies when justified, governed, supportable, and interoperable.
4. Integrations are replaceable adapters, and tool-specific advantages shall not become the platform architecture implicitly.
5. Patterns and best practices shall be selected in response to concrete requirements, failure modes, delivery semantics, topology, or operational risk—not inserted as context-free decoration.

## Article XIV — Explainability, Transparency, and Continuous Human–AI Challenge

1. Material AI recommendations shall state their rationale, considered context, assumptions, constraints, risks, alternatives, and uncertainty.
2. AI shall conduct relevant **Human–AI Challenge** of consequential ambiguity and unsupported assumptions from within its assigned role.
3. Human participants shall be able to challenge, reject, revise, conditionally approve, approve, defer, or supersede AI recommendations.
4. Challenge shall be evidence-based, proportionate, and role-bounded; it shall not be performed merely to appear intelligent.
5. Generated artifacts shall identify provenance and review status.
6. Humans shall be able to understand why an action was proposed and what would be affected.

## Article XV — Security, Identity, Privacy, and Least Authority

1. Security-by-design shall begin with Initiative Classification, architecture, data, interfaces, trust boundaries, and assurance—not after implementation.
2. Authentication and authorization shall always be explicitly assessed for each applicable implementation unit before implementation.
3. A Not Applicable authentication decision shall be recorded with rationale when material.
4. Authorization shall be enforced at the authoritative service, resource, data, or infrastructure boundary, not only in a UI or client.
5. Agents and integrations shall receive only the data and authority required for their task.
6. Sensitive information shall be classified and handled according to policy.
7. External transmission, destructive action, and production change require explicit authorization appropriate to risk.
8. Secrets shall not be embedded in artifacts, prompts, logs, context packs, or generated code.

## Article XVI — Small, Verifiable Progress

1. Work shall be decomposed into the smallest coherent increments that can be reviewed and validated.
2. Each increment shall have a clear objective, bounded change surface, expected output, and evidence.
3. Large opaque generation is disfavored when smaller governed slices are practical.
4. Progress shall be measured by approved value and evidence, not volume of generated content.

## Article XVII — Long-Term Stewardship

1. GAEP shall be designed to evolve across tools, teams, products, non-product initiatives, and years.
2. Architecture, security, assurance, and operational assets shall remain living, versioned, and synchronized with relevant implementation change.
3. Extensibility shall be intentional; speculative complexity shall be resisted.
4. Foundational semantics shall remain stable while adapters and implementations evolve.
5. Decisions shall balance current value, operability, portability, and future change.
6. The platform shall challenge both under-engineering and over-engineering.

## Article XVIII — Adaptive Engineering Alignment

The [Adaptive Engineering Principles](006_ADAPTIVE_ENGINEERING_PRINCIPLES.md) define the authoritative Foundation interpretation of Engineering Initiative neutrality, Applicability Decisions, Technology Profiles, Architecture Assets, Assurance Strategy, Human–AI Challenge, Organizational Boilerplates, Risk-Proportionate Governance, and Change-Driven Re-evaluation.

The [Dynamic Engineering Model](../02_Platform/019_DYNAMIC_ENGINEERING_MODEL.md) resolves applicable engineering work, and the [Engineering Assurance and Architecture Model](../02_Platform/020_ENGINEERING_ASSURANCE_AND_ARCHITECTURE_MODEL.md) governs detailed assurance and architecture execution. These lower-level models operationalize but cannot amend this Constitution.

## Constitutional Compliance Test

A proposal is constitutionally acceptable only if reviewers can answer yes to all applicable questions:

- Is the Engineering Initiative classified and owned?
- Are lifecycle activities and artifacts based on explicit applicability?
- Are human accountability and Explicit Approval clear?
- Is relevant repository context used?
- Are applicable architecture, Technology Profiles, assurance, identity, authorization, and Organizational Boilerplate decisions resolved before implementation?
- Are provenance, traceability, Test Evidence, and Change-Driven Re-evaluation preserved?
- Is AI challenge explainable, relevant, and role-bounded?
- Is vendor, technology, architecture, tool, and test-methodology neutrality preserved or is a constraint consciously approved?
- Are security, privacy, and least authority respected?
- Is governance proportional without omitting necessary rigor?
- Is the design maintainable without context-free complexity?

## Amendment Procedure

Constitutional amendments require:

1. a written amendment proposal and motivation;
2. analysis of affected principles and downstream documents;
3. alternatives and compatibility impact;
4. review by engineering, architecture, governance, quality, security, product, and other authorities as applicable;
5. Explicit Approval by the designated GAEP constitutional owner;
6. a synchronization, migration, and communication plan;
7. a new version with retained history.

An implementation, agent, conversation, or lower-level document cannot amend the Constitution by behavior.
