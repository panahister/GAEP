# Project Vision

**Governed AI Engineering Platform (GAEP)**\
**Document ID:** GAEP-FND-002\
**Version:** 1.0\
**Status:** Draft\
**Authority:** Engineering platform vision

## Purpose

This document describes the future GAEP intends to create. The Constitution defines durable law; this Vision describes what governed, AI-assisted engineering should feel like when those laws are realized.

## The Problem

AI can already generate requirements, diagrams, journeys, designs, backlog items, code, tests, and documentation. Yet organizations continue to experience:

- fragmented product and engineering knowledge;
- repeated explanation of the same context;
- inconsistent decisions across teams and tools;
- isolated artifacts with weak lineage;
- architecture and assurance that drift during implementation;
- duplicated or unofficial boilerplates and practices;
- approval that is implicit or unverifiable;
- loss of knowledge when people or chat sessions disappear;
- increased output without proportional confidence.

AI increases the rate of creation. It does not automatically create organizational coherence.

## Vision Statement

> GAEP transforms repositories into governed engineering workspaces where organizational knowledge, accountable human expertise, and artificial intelligence continuously collaborate to create and evolve Engineering Initiatives with evidence and integrity.

## An Initiative-Neutral Future

GAEP supports products, services, platforms, integrations, infrastructure, libraries, migrations, security improvements, modernization, refactoring, defect fixes, data or AI capabilities, and other technical initiatives.

Product remains an important initiative type. Product discovery, business architecture, user journeys, backlog engineering, UI design, and Figma may be essential for a product initiative. They are not universal prerequisites for a library, worker, backend service, migration, security remediation, or infrastructure change.

The mature platform begins with an **Initiative Classification** and scope, affected implementation units, dependencies, risk, and current state. It then selects applicable lifecycle activities, Architecture Assets, Assurance Strategy, security depth, Organizational Boilerplates, commands, skills, and approvals.

## The Future Experience

When GAEP reaches maturity, a team begins with intent rather than an empty prompt. The workspace understands the Engineering Initiative's purpose, scope, vocabulary, stakeholders, constraints, approved decisions, architecture, applicable lifecycle state, and unresolved risks.

An engineer can express an outcome such as `analyze-impact`, `design-architecture`, `assess-authorization`, `select-test-methodology`, or `prepare-release`. GAEP assembles the minimum trusted context, invokes the appropriate capability, enforces state and Explicit Approval rules, and records resulting artifacts, challenges, decisions, Test Evidence, and trace links.

AI does not start from zero. It starts from governed understanding.

## Repository as Engineering Memory

The mature repository contains more than source code. It retains, as applicable:

- initiative identity, Initiative Classification, scope, glossary, capabilities, and current state;
- Applicability Decisions, requirements, quality attributes, and acceptance criteria;
- domains, modules, actors, roles, permissions, and boundaries;
- processes, rules, data concepts, events, and integrations;
- journeys, UX decisions, design systems, and prototypes;
- epics, features, stories, acceptance criteria, and backlog mappings;
- Architecture Decisions, Architecture Assets, Technology Profiles, contracts, implementation, and Test Evidence;
- identity, authentication, authorization, trust, and security decisions;
- Assurance Strategy, Test Methodology Decisions, test cases, coverage expectations, and quality gates;
- approved Organizational Boilerplates and reusable assets;
- releases, operational observations, incidents, and lessons;
- assumptions, decisions, approvals, exceptions, and change history.

This connected memory enables continuity across time, teams, AI systems, and delivery phases.

## Adaptive Engineering

Every applicable activity participates in a recognizable operating model:

**Understand → Analyze → Design → Review → Approve → Baseline → Trace → Evolve**

Teams can move iteratively, run activities in parallel, reuse approved assets, and revisit earlier work. A phase may be Required, Recommended, Optional, Not Applicable, Deferred, Conditionally Required, Already Satisfied, Reused, Blocked, or Awaiting Human Decision. **Risk-Proportionate Governance** determines the required depth. The platform preserves rationale, state, authority, evidence, and impact relationships so adaptation does not become uncontrolled omission.

## Architecture-Aware and Assurance-Aware Assistance

AI assistance operates against intentional architecture and test-first assurance. Applicable boundaries, topology, data ownership, identity, constraints, and quality attributes are resolved before implementation. Architecture Assets are repository-visible, reviewed, versioned, and updated through Change-Driven Re-evaluation.

Testing is designed with the initiative rather than added after coding. GAEP resolves an Assurance Strategy, Test Methodology Decision, acceptance conditions, applicable test levels, automation expectations, multidimensional coverage, security and authorization scenarios, Test Evidence, and required human approval before implementation readiness.

## Multiple Technologies, One Governed System

GAEP does not prescribe one stack. Different clients, services, modules, workloads, or deployment units may use different technologies when justified. Each major implementation unit has a **Technology Profile** that records its language, framework, material dependencies, operational model, interoperability, rationale, approved Organizational Boilerplate, and approval state.

Heterogeneity is neither required nor prohibited. Enterprise standards may constrain choices, but the platform evaluates fitness at the correct component level.

## Continuous Human–AI Collaboration

Humans contribute:

- purpose and values;
- business understanding;
- ethical and strategic judgment;
- creativity and negotiation;
- risk ownership;
- accountable approval.

AI contributes, within its assigned role:

- rapid synthesis and analysis;
- consistency checking;
- generation and transformation;
- trace and impact discovery;
- validation and role-bounded Human–AI Challenge;
- knowledge retrieval and reuse.

GAEP succeeds when each participant challenges and amplifies the other without obscuring responsibility. Humans may reject, revise, conditionally approve, approve, defer, or supersede AI recommendations. Consequential outcomes require Explicit Approval; silence is not approval.

## From Prompts to Intent

Prompt engineering is a temporary interface. The long-term model is command and context engineering:

- users express intent through stable commands or workflows;
- commands declare the capability and context contract;
- the platform loads current, relevant, authoritative knowledge;
- specialized skills apply repeatable methods;
- agents reason and act inside policy and state boundaries;
- results return to the repository with provenance and evidence.

Prompts may still exist internally, but they are implementation details rather than the organizational operating model.

## Connected, Conditional Lifecycle

GAEP connects the reasoning chain that applies to the initiative:

Initiative and objective → classification and applicability → requirements and constraints → architecture, security, and Technology Profiles → acceptance conditions and Assurance Strategy → Organizational Boilerplates and implementation readiness → implementation and Test Evidence → release, migration, or operational outcome → learning and Change-Driven Re-evaluation

Product-specific chains may additionally include capability, value stream, domain, process, journey, UX, Figma, backlog, and product acceptance. These remain connected without becoming universal.

Users can navigate in both directions: from a business objective to production evidence, or from a code or operational change back to its originating intent.

## Continuous Evolution

Engineering Initiatives do not merely move through a lifecycle once. Products and non-product assets evolve continuously. GAEP remembers:

- what changed and why;
- the request and affected artifacts;
- the options considered;
- who reviewed and approved;
- which architecture, security, tests, coverage, and Test Evidence validated the change;
- what was learned after release.

This makes change explainable and organizational learning cumulative.

## Platform, Not Tool Lock-In

Models, IDEs, frameworks, and cloud platforms will change. GAEP keeps its core semantics stable:

- governance;
- artifact and state models;
- approval and traceability;
- context contracts;
- command and skill contracts;
- evidence and change history.

Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, AI-DLC, and future systems are integrations or execution options. Organizations can adopt new tools without abandoning their engineering memory or operating rules.

## Target Outcomes

GAEP aims to produce measurable improvement in:

- time required to establish trusted initiative context;
- consistency and completeness of engineering artifacts;
- trace coverage across the lifecycle;
- reuse of approved Organizational Boilerplates and other assets;
- review quality and decision transparency;
- architecture conformance;
- defect and rework reduction;
- onboarding speed;
- auditability and operational learning;
- safe adoption of new AI capabilities.

## Success Criteria

GAEP is succeeding when:

1. Teams no longer re-explain foundational initiative context to every AI tool.
2. Approved knowledge remains current, discoverable, and attributable.
3. AI output consistently references relevant constraints and upstream intent.
4. Material artifacts and decisions have meaningful trace relationships.
5. Human approvals are explicit, version-bound, visible, and proportional to risk.
6. Architecture and implementation drift are detected early.
7. Reusable assets improve across products and non-product initiatives without becoming context-free rules.
8. Tool replacement does not erase organizational memory.
9. Delivery becomes faster without sacrificing evidence or accountability.
10. Teams trust GAEP because it exposes uncertainty and respects authority.

## Non-Goals

The vision does not require:

- full autonomy;
- elimination of professional roles;
- a single mandatory development methodology;
- generation of every possible artifact;
- Product Discovery, Figma, UI, data, messaging, or distributed patterns for every initiative;
- one programming language, framework, database, cache, broker, architecture style, or test methodology for every component;
- AI-generated official Organizational Boilerplates;
- architecture or test assurance performed only after implementation;
- centralized control of every local team decision;
- premature implementation of all platform capabilities.
